Home Rental Platform | Product Structure & Functional Requirements  
1. Executive Summary 
The application is an owner-based, subscription SaaS platform for rental-property management. The platform 
administrator creates and manages owner accounts. Each owner receives a private management dashboard and an 
owner-branded public website with its own shareable link or connected custom domain. The homes and rooms 
published by that owner appear on that owner’s website, rather than being mixed into one mandatory shared 
marketplace. Renters browse that owner’s listings, contact the owner through email, WhatsApp, or in-app chat, apply for 
a home, complete the rental process, upload requested verification documents, pay rent through supported methods, 
and raise requests during their tenancy. Owners manage multiple properties, multiple units, shared occupancy, monthly 
rent, tenant documents, requests, and analytics from their dashboard. Owners pay a recurring subscription to use the 
service; the platform administrator manages plans, billing status, limits, domains, and account provisioning. 
The platform should distinguish a property (for example, a building or house) from a rentable unit (for example, an 
apartment, room, or bed). This matters when one owner has several houses or when several tenants share a single 
property. 
Owner-Based SaaS Model (Core Product Decision) 
This product is not primarily a single public rental marketplace. It is a multi-tenant SaaS platform where each landlord or 
property owner operates their own rental website, powered by the same central application. The platform 
operator/admin controls account creation and subscription administration; each owner controls only their own brand, 
listings, tenants, payment settings, and property operations. 
Platform administrator workflow 
 Admin creates an owner account and enters the owner’s name, email, initial plan, subscription status, allowed 
property/unit limits, and onboarding status. 
 The system sends the owner a secure invitation to activate the account, set a password, verify email, and complete 
profile/business details. 
 The admin assigns or confirms the owner’s website address: for example, ownername.platformdomain.com, or a 
custom domain such as rentals.ownerbrand.com. 
 The admin can activate, suspend, upgrade, downgrade, or cancel an owner account according to the subscription 
and platform policies. 
 Admin can view subscription/payment status and technical health, but should not routinely access private tenant 
documents or owner communications without an authorised support reason and audit log. 
Owner workspace and branded website 
 Each owner signs in to a private dashboard to manage properties, units, listings, tenant applications, active 
tenancies, rent, documents, messages, requests, and analytics. 
 Each owner has a public mini-site showing only that owner’s published listings, logo/brand name, approved contact 
details, description, and relevant rental policies. 
 The owner can share their unique URL directly with prospective tenants. A visitor using that URL should see the 
relevant owner’s homes only by default. 
 A central platform homepage may explain the SaaS product and allow owners to sign in, but it is separate from 
each owner’s public rental website. 
 An optional platform-wide directory/search can be added later, but it must not be required for the owner-based 
model to work. 
Subscription and plan management 
 Owners subscribe to use the platform, with monthly and/or annual billing. Plan options may limit active properties, 
rentable units, staff accounts, storage, custom domains, or advanced reports. 
 Keep subscription billing separate from tenant rent collection. The owner’s subscription is paid to the platform 
operator; rent is paid by tenants to the owner or the owner’s configured payment recipient.

Multi-tenant data isolation 
 Every owner-owned record must carry an owner/tenant scope or be reachable only through a parent record that is

 Every backend request must verify that the signed-in user is allowed to access the requested owner, property, unit,

 Use automated tests to confirm Owner A cannot read or change Owner B’s data by changing IDs, URLs, filters, or 
API payloads. 
 For custom domains, determine the public site owner from a verified hostname mapping, but determine private 
dashboard access from authenticated identity and permissions. 
 Use audit logs for admin impersonation/support access, subscription changes, domain changes, payment 
adjustments, and sensitive document review. 
Important financial separation 
 Platform subscription invoices are issued by the SaaS operator to property owners. 
 Tenant rent charges and receipts belong to the relevant owner/tenancy and must not be counted as SaaS 
subscription revenue. 
 Owner payout/bank settings are configured separately for each owner. The system must not route rent from one 
owner to another owner. 
 If the platform ever collects tenant rent and distributes it to owners, assess payment-provider marketplace 
capabilities and legal/regulatory obligations before implementation. 
2. Product Goals 
 Give owners one place to manage all properties, listings, tenants, rent, documents, and requests. 
 Help renters search and compare homes, ask questions, apply, and manage their tenancy. 
 Support shared accommodation where multiple tenants can be associated with one property or unit. 
 Make rent status transparent: due, paid, partially paid, overdue, disputed, or waived. 
 Keep sensitive identity and tenancy documents private, access-controlled, and auditable. 
 Provide clear operational analytics without presenting unconfirmed payment attempts as completed payments.

Important modelling rule: do not treat “house” and “tenant” as a simple one-to-one relationship. A property can contain 
multiple units, and a unit or shared tenancy may have multiple tenants. Store the actual rental arrangement in a tenancy 
record and connect tenants through a tenant-membership table. This avoids problems when someone moves in or out. 
6. End-to-End User Workflows 
6.1 Owner publishes a home 
1. Owner creates an account and completes required owner verification. 
2. Owner adds a property with address, property type, amenities, photos, rules, and ownership/management details. 
3. Owner creates one or more rentable units and sets rent, deposit, utilities, availability date, occupancy limits, and 
rental terms. 
4. Owner previews and publishes a listing. The listing becomes searchable only when it passes required checks. 
5. Owner can pause, edit, mark unavailable, or archive a listing; changes are reflected in renter search. 
6.2 Renter discovers and contacts owner 
6. Renter searches by location, budget, move-in date, property type, rooms, furnished status, and other filters. 
7. Renter opens a listing and reviews photos, price breakdown, amenities, rules, availability, and owner profile. 
8. Renter can save the listing and contact the owner by in-app chat, email, or WhatsApp. 
9. Contact actions should preserve context by linking the conversation or inquiry to the specific listing. 
10. If the renter proceeds, they submit an application or viewing request; the owner can accept, reject, or request more 
information. 
6.3 Booking, agreement, and move-in 
11. Owner and renter agree on the unit, move-in date, rent, deposit, payment schedule, and tenancy terms.

11. Payment Architecture: Wise and Bank Accounts 
Payment design should be validated before implementation because “pay rent from Wise or any bank account” can 
mean different things. The app can show an owner’s verified receiving details and let tenants make a bank transfer, or it 
can use a payment provider that supports the required payment method, market, currency, and payout model. Wise 
availability and API permissions depend on the account type, country, and product; do not assume a Wise account can 
be debited directly by the app. 
 MVP option: display verified owner bank-transfer details, create a unique payment reference for each rent charge, 
let the tenant mark a transfer as sent, and reconcile it against bank/provider data or owner confirmation. Label this 
as “awaiting confirmation” until verified. 
 Integrated option: use a payment service provider that supports the required country, tenant payment methods, 
marketplace/landlord payouts, refunds, and webhook events. 
 Store provider tokens/references rather than online banking passwords. Never ask tenants to submit bank login 
credentials. 
 Verify webhook signatures, make webhook processing idempotent, and preserve raw event metadata safely for 
reconciliation. 
 Define who legally receives funds, who pays provider fees, how refunds/deposits work, and how disputes are 
handled before launch. 
 Show separate statuses for rent charge, payment attempt, and owner payout. A successful tenant payment does 
not necessarily mean the owner payout has settled. 
12. Security, Privacy, and Compliance Considerations 
 Use role-based access control and property/tenancy-level authorization on every API request. 
 Encrypt traffic with HTTPS and encrypt sensitive files and secrets at rest; use a secrets manager or environment 
secrets, not committed credentials. 
 Store verification files in private object storage and provide short-lived signed URLs only after permission checks. 
 Log document views/downloads and sensitive owner/admin actions; avoid putting personal document contents into 
ordinary application logs. 
 Apply file size/type limits, malware scanning where feasible, rate limiting, input validation, secure session handling, 
and backup/restore testing. 
 Define retention, deletion, consent, access, and correction workflows for personal data. For a Germany/EU launch, 
obtain appropriate legal advice on GDPR, rental practices, payment regulation, consumer information, and required 
disclosures. 
 Do not collect or expose more personal information than needed for a clear rental purpose. Avoid displaying one 
tenant’s private documents to other co-tenants. 
 Have clear policies for deposits, late fees, cancellations, disputes, maintenance emergencies, and owner/tenant 
responsibilities.

workflows are stable. The backend should remain the single source of truth for web and mobile clients.

Keep the MVP focused. Payment integrations, legal rental workflows, and document handling carry more risk than basic 
listing screens, so validate these early with a small end-to-end prototype. 
17. Non-Functional Requirements 
 Responsive UI for desktop, tablet, and mobile; accessible forms and clear empty/error/loading states. 
 Secure authorization enforced server-side; frontend visibility rules are not security controls. 
 Reliable rent ledger with immutable transaction history and reconciliation capability. 
 Idempotent payment/webhook processing and duplicate-safe recurring job execution. 
 Backups, restore tests, monitoring, alerting, and documented incident response. 
 Search results should be paginated and indexed; large analytics queries should not block critical rent/payment 
operations. 
 All dates stored consistently (UTC timestamps for events, explicit local date/time rules for rent due dates); currency 
stored with each charge. 
 Audit records for important status transitions, document reviews, rent adjustments, and manual payment 
confirmation.

19. Decisions to Confirm Before Development 
 Launch market and legal setup: Germany only at first, or multiple countries? 
 Property structure: whole homes only, or rooms/shared accommodation and individual units from day one? 
 Rent responsibility: one payment per tenancy, or separate rent shares/payments for each co-tenant? 
 Payments: bank transfer with reconciliation first, or an integrated payment provider from launch? Which currencies 
are required? 
 Owner payout model: direct payment to each owner’s account, or platform collection followed by payouts? This 
changes the legal and technical design. 
 Documents: exactly which document types are needed, who reviews them, and how long they are retained? 
 Rental agreement: upload an existing agreement, use an e-signature provider, or keep signing outside the app in 
the MVP? 
 Contact policy: should phone/email/WhatsApp be visible immediately, or only after an inquiry/application? 
 Will property managers/agents have delegated access in the first release? 
 Existing database and codebase: continue the current MongoDB implementation or switch to PostgreSQL before 
the schema grows?
